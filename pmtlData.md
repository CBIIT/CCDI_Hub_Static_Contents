The development of safe and effective therapies for children with cancer requires early and intentional planning in drug development. Federal policies, Best Pharmaceuticals for Children Act (BPCA) and the Pediatric Research Equity Act (PREA), support the evaluation of oncology products in pediatric populations. As part of this effort, the Pediatric Molecular Target Lists (PMTL) was developed by the Food and Drug Administration (FDA), with input from the National Cancer Institute and the pediatric cancer research community, to identify molecular targets relevant to pediatric cancers.

The Pediatric Molecular Target Lists (PMTL) include two categories:

1. [Relevant Molecular Targets](https://www.fda.gov/media/161463/download?attachment) – Targets with scientific evidence (such as gene expression data, genetic alterations, or published research) suggesting they may play a role in the growth or progression of pediatric cancers.
2. [Non-Relevant Molecular Targets (Leading to Waiver)](https://www.fda.gov/media/161462/download?attachment) – Targets with evidence indicating they are not associated with pediatric tumor growth and may therefore qualify for waiver of early pediatric assessment.

These lists are updated regularly as new scientific data becomes available and are posted on the [FDA Oncology Center of Excellence website](https://www.fda.gov/about-fda/oncology-center-excellence/pediatric-oncology). They fulfill statutory requirements under [FDARA](https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/fdara-implementation-guidance-pediatric-studies-molecularly-targeted-oncology-drugs-amendments-sec) and help guide industry in planning initial Pediatric Study Plans for oncology drugs and biologics in development, consistent with PREA.
